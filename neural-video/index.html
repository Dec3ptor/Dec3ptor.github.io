Neural video codec

Pick a video. A small neural network is trained, in your browser, to memorise that one clip: it maps a timestamp to a frame of pixels. Once trained, the video is thrown away and playback runs entirely out of the network's weights. The weights are the file. Whether that is good compression is what the numbers at the bottom are for.

What is actually happening

The network is f(t) → frame: a timestamp goes through a Fourier feature embedding and a small MLP, which produces a low-resolution feature map; convolution + pixel-shuffle blocks upsample that 32× into RGB. Training is pure overfitting on a single clip — there is no dataset and no generalisation. Afterwards the weights are quantised to 8 bits per value, which is what the reported file size measures.

This is the NeRV family of implicit neural representations. There is a command-line version with proper entropy coding and an x264 benchmark in nvc.py next to this page.
