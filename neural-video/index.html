Neural video codec

Pick a video. A small neural network is trained, in your browser, to memorise that one clip: it maps a timestamp to a frame of pixels. Once trained, the video is thrown away and playback runs entirely out of the network's weights. The weights are the file. Whether that is good compression is what the numbers at the bottom are for.

What is actually happening

The network is f(t) → frame, and the two architectures differ only in where the per-frame code comes from. nerv generates it: the timestamp goes through a Fourier feature embedding and a small MLP that produces a low-resolution feature map. grid stores it instead, as a learned stack of feature maps interpolated in time, plus a second finer grid injected partway up the decoder. Either way, convolution + pixel-shuffle blocks upsample 32× into RGB, and training is pure overfitting on one clip — no dataset, no generalisation. The weights are then quantised to 8 bits, which is what the reported size measures.

Which architecture wins depends on the footage: at a matched parameter budget, grid measured 2.43 dB ahead on a textured pan and 1.36 dB behind on smooth gradients, so try both. The command-line version next to this page (nvc.py) adds quantisation-aware training, a truncatable bitstream, and an x264 benchmark that is honest about how far off this all still is — roughly 6 to 8 times the bytes at matched quality.
